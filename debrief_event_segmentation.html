What are we trying to learn in this research?

This is an exploratory study investigating if there are “meaningful moments” when viewing naturalistic videos, and how they are represented and remembered. A side research question is to investigate how similar/dissimilar such moments are to event boundaries. Event boundaries are naturally occuring breaks between two meaningful units of our comprehension. They have been shown to play a crucial role in how we represent and remember our daily life experiences. The stimuli used in this study are also being used in other studies posted online.

Why is this important to scientists or the general public?

Using complex, naturalistic stimuli such as movies has gained popularity among researchers in recent years. However, due to their complexity, it can be challenging to quantify subjective values such as “importance.” Such values are also highly relevant to behavioral measures, such as recall probability, and therefore developing a method to measure them would be very beneficial in research that uses movies. This experiment attempts to validate the utility of this paradigm for quantifying subjective values that fluctuate across a movie.

What are our hypothesis and predictions?

We hypothesize that the identified key moments will be frequently recalled when participants recount the movie after viewing. We also hypothesize that the key moments are the most essential for understanding the story and updating evaluations of the main characters in the movie. In other words, these key moments convey more information than other moments/events in the movie.
